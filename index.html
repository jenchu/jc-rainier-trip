Pregnancy-Friendly Itinerary

Every hike on this trip is easy, short, and on well-maintained trails — specifically chosen so everyone can participate fully. Sol Duc Falls (1.6 mi, minimal gain), Hall of Mosses (0.8 mi loop), and Marymere Falls (1.8 mi round trip) are all family-friendly and suitable for second-trimester hikers. The beaches require short walks on flat or slightly uneven terrain. Consult your OB before the trip with any specific questions.

Oak Table Cafe · Sequim

A beloved local brunch spot in Sequim, about 1.5 hours from SEA. Famous for their apple pancake and casual Pacific Northwest charm. Great first meal to decompress from travel. Sequim is a cute small town worth a short stroll after eating — it sits in a rare rain shadow and often gets sunshine even when the rest of the peninsula is overcast.

💧

~2:30 PM

Lake Crescent · Marymere Falls

Stunning glacially-carved lake right on Hwy 101 — one of the most beautiful spots in the park and you barely have to leave the car to see it. Walk the Marymere Falls trail: 1.8 miles round trip through old-growth forest to a gorgeous 90-foot waterfall. Well-maintained, easy terrain, suitable for everyone. Spend about 1.5 hours here. Skip Devil's Punchbowl — it requires scrambling and is not suitable for the pregnant traveler.

1.8 mi round trip Pregnancy-friendly

~4:30 PM

Rialto Beach

Only 15 minutes from Forks — a perfect first taste of the Olympic coast. Park and walk directly onto the beach within minutes. Dramatic sea stacks, crashing surf, and enormous driftwood logs tossed onto the shore. Walk north as far as the group wants. Note: Mora Road is open for your dates — a full closure begins July 8, so you're in the clear. Check tide charts before going for the best beach walking. Spend 1–1.5 hours.

⚠️ Arrive Hoh before 9am
🌿

~8:30 AM – 11:00 AM

Hoh Rainforest · Hall of Mosses + Spruce Nature Trail

One of the most otherworldly places in the continental US. Do both trails back to back: the Hall of Mosses (0.8 mi loop) winds through massive bigleaf maple trees draped in thick club moss — the canopy glows an impossible shade of green. Then the Spruce Nature Trail (1.2 mi loop) takes you through ancient Sitka spruce forest alongside the Hoh River. Total: about 2 miles of easy walking, ~1.5–2 hours. Keep an eye out for Roosevelt elk — they frequently graze right at the trailhead. Trail surfaces are hard-packed with some tree roots; go slowly and watch your footing.

~2 mi total · Easy Pregnancy-friendly
🌳

~11:30 AM

Tree of Life · Kalaloch Beach

~20 minutes south of Hoh on Hwy 101. A massive Sitka spruce growing suspended over an undercut coastal cliff, its roots fully exposed and dangling in the air — it should have fallen long ago but somehow keeps growing. One of the strangest and most photogenic sights on the peninsula. 5-minute walk from the Kalaloch Beach parking area.

5-min walk Iconic stop
🏖️

~12:00 PM

Ruby Beach

~10 minutes further south on Hwy 101. One of the most dramatic and photogenic beaches in the Pacific Northwest — sea stacks rise directly from the shore, driftwood logs carpet the beach, and Cedar Creek flows into the Pacific right at the beach entrance. The parking area and overlook are fully accessible; the trail down to the beach is short but has a moderate slope. Spend 45–60 minutes. Best at low tide for tide pool exploring.

Best beach on the peninsula
🏡

~2:30 PM

~1 hour back to Forks. Relax, shower, cook dinner. Early night — tomorrow is an early start and you're checking out.

~8:30 AM – 10:30 AM

Sol Duc Falls · THE PRIORITY HIKE

The centerpiece of the trip. 1.6 miles round trip on a wide, well-maintained trail through dense old-growth rainforest — every shade of green, towering Douglas firs and western hemlocks draped in moss. The falls themselves are spectacular: the Sol Duc River splits into multiple channels as it thunders 48 feet into a narrow rocky canyon. A historic wooden shelter sits right at the viewpoint. Minimal elevation gain — suitable for all fitness levels.

1.6 mi · Easy Pregnancy-friendly
🦪

~1:00 – 2:30 PM

Hama Hama Oyster Saloon · Hood Canal

~1.5 hours from Sol Duc north on Hwy 101 along Hood Canal. The perfect send-off. A fifth-generation family oyster farm with private A-frame picnic tables overlooking the water. Order fresh raw and grilled oysters, steamed clams, and local beer or wine. Open Friday–Sunday 11am–5pm. Reserve via Tock — $75/table includes two dozen oysters. Reservations open every Monday at 9am for the upcoming weekend. Walk-ins welcome but summer waits can be 1+ hour.

📅 Book on Tock — do it Monday

Before you go

Good to Know

🎫

Park Entry

$35/vehicle for a 7-day Olympic National Park pass. America the Beautiful annual pass ($80) covers it and is worth buying if anyone plans more national park visits this year.

🦪

Hama Hama Reservation

Open Fri–Sun only, 11am–5pm. Book via Tock — reservations open every Monday at 9am for the upcoming weekend. $75/table includes two dozen oysters and a private A-frame. Book this Monday for June 6.

🅿️

Hoh Parking

Parking fills by 10am on summer weekends and waits can be 1–2 hours. Arrive before 9am — non-negotiable on a Friday in June.

🌊

Tide Charts

Check tide charts for Rialto Beach (June 4) and Ruby Beach (June 5) before heading out. Low tide reveals tide pools and far more beach to explore. Use tideschart.com or the NPS app.

🚗

Cars & Groceries

Rent 1–2 cars at SEA. Grocery shop at Fred Meyer in Port Angeles on Day 1 — Forks only has a small Thriftway. All roads are standard paved highways, no 4WD needed.

📡

Cell Service

Very limited inside the park and around Forks. Download offline Google Maps before leaving Seattle. The NPS app has offline park maps and trail info too. Save this page before you lose signal.

🌧️

Rain Gear

The Hoh gets 140+ inches of rain per year. Even in June, the rainforest will be damp. A proper waterproof jacket (not just water-resistant) is essential for Day 3. Waterproof boots strongly recommended.

🦌

Wildlife

Roosevelt elk are frequently spotted at Hoh — often right at the trailhead. Keep your distance and don't approach. Salmon at Sol Duc Cascades are best in early morning. Keep an eye out for bald eagles at the coast.
